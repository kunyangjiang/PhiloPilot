## 2. Central philosophical arguments

- Consent is justified in several ways, and the justification chosen determines what is owed. A "protection only" justification would let doctor and family withhold the truth; an autonomy or trust justification would not.
- A patient choosing not to know is an autonomous act. Family deciding without asking is a substitute decision that needs strong justification.
- Abortion: Marquis's deprivation argument is set against Thomson's bodily-autonomy argument and Warren's personhood criteria. Each is reconstructed in numbered steps and given its strongest objection.
- End of life: refusing treatment, requesting assisted death, and non-voluntary or involuntary ending of life are three different moral questions. The chapter refuses to slide from one to the next.
- Allocation: no single principle is adequate, and procedural fairness matters when substantive principles conflict.
- Comparison: Confucian filial respect and Ubuntu relational personhood are used to ask *how* family takes part. They do not replace the patient's consent.

## 5. Comparative methodology

Section 六 follows D1's rules. It states why another tradition is being asked (to see how the family takes part, not whether the patient's consent counts). It keeps each tradition's historical position apart from the chapter's application, marked ［本章的重建］ and ［本章的判断］. It compares one case under two approaches in a table that also lists what each approach leaves out. The Buddhist debate in 四 is presented as an internal disagreement between scholars, not as "the Buddhist view".

## 8. Component (`ui/media/d8/CaseApproaches.tsx`)

- **Anchor:** in 六, after the paragraph beginning "第三，保护病人不受强迫".
- **What the reader does:**
  - Picks one of 宋秀琴's three decisions: telling her the diagnosis, the feeding tube, or her request to "go early".
  - Predicts whether the individual-centred and the family- or relation-centred approaches agree (same conclusion and reasons, close conclusions with different reasons, or different conclusions).
  - Then sees the chapter's two table cells and its own remark.
  - A second step walks the chapter's seven-step method on the same decision, with textareas.
- **Rationale:** the chapter's central method (one case, two approaches, then structured case reasoning) is something the reader should *do*, not just read.
- **Constraints:** scores nothing and declares neither approach correct; every reading comes from the chapter's own table and remarks.
- **State:** saved under `philopilot.v2.D8.media.bioethics-case-approaches`.
