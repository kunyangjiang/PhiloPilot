## 2. Important arguments

- **Bentham:** the fact/norm double claim in "two sovereign masters" and the gap it leaves (table in 一); the seven dimensions of the calculus; animals ("Can they suffer?").
- **Mill:** qualitative pleasures and the competent judges; the "proof" (*Utilitarianism* ch. 4) and its standard objections; act/rule.
- **Sidgwick:** "the point of view of the universe" and the dualism of practical reason.
- **Harm principle:** which reasons qualify, not when intervention is obligatory; utility "in the largest sense".
- **Taylor Mill:** no portion of the species may decide the proper sphere of another; the argument is epistemic (one cannot know capacities under exclusion).
- **Peirce:** doubt and belief; the maxim; the untested diamond; truth as the opinion fated to be agreed by all who investigate.
- **James:** the squirrel; truth as verification and "the expedient", with his qualifications; a table separating practical consequences, inquiry, warranted belief and truth.
- **Dewey:** inquiry as a process from an indeterminate situation; democracy and education as continuous with it.
- **Addams:** social ethics learned on "the thronged and common road"; the charity visitor's predicament; "lateral" progress.
- **Du Bois:** double consciousness; the function of the university.
