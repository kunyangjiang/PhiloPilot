## 2. Important arguments

- **Post-Kantian problems:** a first principle (Reinhold); Schulze's scepticism, where transcendental arguments show how we must think, not how things are; nature and freedom; a Spinozism of freedom.
- **Fichte:** being and self-awareness are the same for the I. The finite I must find itself limited. The summons gives limitation a new form, another free being, and with it mutual recognition.
- **Schelling:** explaining purposiveness by a divine intellect is "not philosophising". In the organism, nature is free in its blind lawfulness. The late Schelling holds that reason cannot explain why there is something.
- **Hegel's dialectic:** three moments; the opposite comes from a determination's own one-sidedness, not from outside. Being and nothing pass into becoming; becoming sublates both. Evaluation means asking whether a transition is necessary.
- **Recognition:** one-sided recognition fails; labour returns the slave to himself. The section is a structural analysis, not a history.
- **Freedom and institutions (§五, final corrections):** freedom needs an external existence (§29: right as the existence of the free will; §§36, 41: person and property). Property needs recognition, which contract presupposes (§71 Remark), and contract can be broken (wrong, §82ff.), which leads to morality (§§104–105). Morality's rights are real (knowledge §117, intention §120, subjective freedom §124 Remark) but formal; conscience cannot fix its own content (§§135–139). Ethical life is freedom become a world (§142); in duty the individual is liberated (§149). Family, civil society and corporation, and the state as "the actuality of concrete freedom" (§260). Poverty and the rabble (§§244–245) are left unresolved by Hegel. The state is a constitutional monarchy with estates (§§273, 301, 305–308), not a liberal democracy; §258 Remark also says individuals have objectivity only as members of the state. Recognition is kept at three distinct levels (*Phenomenology*; abstract right and morality; ethical life).
- **History:** "progress in the consciousness of freedom" against Romantic "progressive" poetry, which is never complete. Günderrode agrees with Schleiermacher on immediate access to unity but rejects the dependence of world on spirit, holding the two to be polar.

## 9. Unresolved issues

Note numbers are those of the revised manuscript.

1. The Günderrode section (notes 59–61) rests wholly on Herrera. Her own notes (Morgenthaler ed.) were not consulted. The comparison with Hegel's "whole" is the chapter's reconstruction.
2. Caroline Schlegel-Schelling and Dorothea Schlegel appear only by their place in the circle (notes 54, 58).
3. *Athenaeum* fragment 116 is now checked in a 1798 scan (note 55), but its Fraktur text layer is very poor: the quoted German was read word by word and corrected. The fragments are unnumbered in the original; "116" is the later editorial number. No page number is given because the page markers are not reliable.
4. Fichte and Schelling locations are now checked (notes 10, 19, 22). Schelling p. 55 is assigned to the *Ideen* introduction because it precedes the "Erstes Buch" heading; p. 527 to *Von der Weltseele* by the part heading above it. The scan's table of contents was not used.
5. Schulze's three criticisms, the pantheism controversy, the atheism controversy, wirklich vs vorhanden, the textual history of the history lectures, and Hegel on irony and on Africa rest on general overviews.
6. Whether "all cows are black" targets Schelling himself or his followers is disputed; the chapter says only "通常认为".
7. The *Philosophy of Right* reconstruction is one reading of the work (notes 39–47). Whether Hegel's transitions are necessary is disputed. Judgements on poverty, the monarchy and conservative vs progressive readings follow Brooks (SEP); the studies Brooks cites were not read. The German was checked in Lasson 1911, not in the 1820 first edition. Gans's *Zusätze* were not used.
