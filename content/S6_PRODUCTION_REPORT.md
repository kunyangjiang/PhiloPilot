## 5. Comparisons

Two six-question comparisons, each labelled （本章的比较） and each with a "where the analogy fails" row:

1. **Apoha and family resemblance (四).** Both explain how one word covers many individuals without a shared essence. The analogy fails in three places:
   - Dharmakīrti's particulars are momentary, a metaphysics Wittgenstein lacks;
   - *apoha* is a systematic theory serving Buddhist epistemology, while Wittgenstein distrusts philosophical theory;
   - Dharmakīrti's "similarity" is causal similarity of mental images, while Wittgenstein's is similarity visible in public practice.
2. **The Mohist 狗/犬 and Frege's morning star / evening star (五).** Both notice that a speaker can know a thing under one name and deny knowing it under another (《经下》 calls this 過). The analogy fails in three places:
   - 狗 and 犬 are synonyms in one language, so learning both needs only the synonymy, whereas the identity of the stars was an empirical discovery;
   - the Mohists' unit is the 名, while Frege's problem concerns the thought and truth value of sentences;
   - a related canon has a textual problem (CN2 §3), so the "Mohist view" can turn on one character.

Both end with "两者没有任何历史联系". The intro separates documented historical links from comparisons built for teaching, giving the Arabic logicians' reading of Aristotle as its example of the first kind.
