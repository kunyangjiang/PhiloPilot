## 2. Central philosophical arguments

- **Anthropocentrism.** Weak anthropocentrism can explain much environmental concern. The "last man" case tests whether it can explain all of it.
- **Speciesism and marginal cases.** Singer's argument from sentience is reconstructed, with the costs it forces (the island goats). Regan's rights view is set beside it, as is the tension between both views and holistic environmental ethics.
- **Holism.** The land ethic values the community. Regan's charge of "environmental fascism" is answered by Callicott's revision: we belong to several communities, and duties to nearer communities usually take priority.
- **Non-identity.** If the reservoir changes who will be born, "harm to future people" needs an impersonal or rights-based reformulation.
- **Climate justice.** Each of the three distributive principles has a standard objection. Shue's distinction limits what may be asked of the poor.
- **Comparison.** Ecocentrism, Daoism and Buddhism all give the fish and the forest more weight than anthropocentrism does, but for different reasons. None of the three answers the question of 江城's water, and none attends to the villagers' graves. The chapter's own judgment (marked ［本章的判断］) names three conditions for a defensible decision.

## 5. Comparative methodology

- Each tradition is read first in its own text. 1A7 concerns the king's compassion, not animal rights. 秋水's "以道观之，物无贵贱" is a claim about perspective. 马蹄 is primarily political allegory, according to standard accounts.
- Only then is the tradition applied to 青溪, under ［本章的应用］.
- The comparison table records where each approach is silent, including the graves.
- Miller's caution against reading Daoism as ready-made environmentalism, and Schmithausen's caution about early Buddhism, are given in the text.

## 8. Component (`ui/media/d9/StandingTester.tsx`)

- **Anchor:** in 三, after the paragraph beginning "这张表说明：同样一个水库".
- **What the reader does:** picks one of five theories (strong anthropocentrism, Singer, Regan, Taylor, Leopold / Callicott) and one of four hard cases from parts 一–三 (the last man, the island goats, germs and weeds, 青溪's fish). The reader predicts the verdict, then sees a reading.
- **Labelling:** 8 of the 20 readings are the chapter's own and are labelled so. The other 12 are written for the exercise from the chapter's account of the theory, and say so.
- **Rationale:** the chapter's method is "test an argument against hard cases". The component lets the reader run that test across the whole theory × case grid instead of only the pairs the text discusses.
- **Constraints:** scores nothing and declares no theory correct.
- **State:** saved under `philopilot.v2.D9.media.standing-tester`.
- **Keyboard:** fully exercised in check 6.
