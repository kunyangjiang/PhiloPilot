## 2. Important arguments

- **Frege:** three principles of the *Grundlagen* (psychological vs logical; context principle; concept vs object); Basic Law V and Russell's 1902 letter (via SEP); the Jupiter example (§§54/57).
- **Sense and reference:** the cognitive value of "a = b"; sense is not an idea; the thought as the sense of a sentence and the truth value as its reference; indirect contexts (left as a standing difficulty).
- **Russell:** three puzzles of "On Denoting" (including "A differs from B", p. 485); his reasons for rejecting Frege ("plainly false", "plainly artificial", pp. 483–484); existence, uniqueness, predication; primary and secondary occurrence; Strawson's objection.
- ***Tractatus*:** picture theory (2.1–2.13, 4.01, 4.024), saying and showing (4.022, 4.1212), the limit drawn in language (preface), 4.003, 6.54 and two readings.
- **Logical empiricism:** the criterion, its versions, its failures, and the reply that it is a proposal.
- ***Investigations*:** the Augustinian picture (§1–2), "meaning is use" with its §43 qualification, family resemblance (§§65–67), the rule-following paradox (§201), private language.
- **Ordinary language:** category mistakes; doing things with words; misfire vs abuse; what is said vs what is conveyed (Grice).
