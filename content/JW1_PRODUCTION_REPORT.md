## 2. Important arguments

- **Saadia:** creation from finitude, composition, accidents and time; the "finitude" argument reconstructed in four steps, with its weak premise (step 3) named. Borrowed kalām forms are kept apart from kalām doctrines: Saadia departs from atomism and continuous re-creation.
- **Maimonides' via negativa:** first, the dilemma of attributes; second, attributes of action; third, the seven-step reconstruction (simplicity, the five kinds of positive attribute, the "shared" predicate, *tasāmuḥ*, negation needing no common genus, the ship, silence as praise). The double-negation objection is answered with the SEP reading (the dog example). The central claim is the difference in epistemic standing: affirmation *claims* knowledge of the essence, while negation only excludes.
- **Creation vs eternity:** three views; Maimonides holds that neither side is demonstrated, but that creation is preferable. He would read Genesis figuratively *if* eternity were proved.
- **Gersonides:** a Platonic-type creation from eternal formless matter, which he claims is demonstrable. God knows particulars only "insofar as ordered" and does not know beforehand which contingent outcome will happen. Attributes are "common in essence, divergent in quantity".
- **Crescas:** his critique of Aristotle's physics (infinity); what can and cannot be proved; determinism kept with commandments (law is itself a cause); love rather than intellect.
- **Karaites (V23 correction):** the dispute is not "reason vs faith" but which transmitted knowledge counts and who may interpret it. Qirqisānī and Yefet accept Saadia's first three sources of knowledge but restrict "true report" to Scripture (table). Rejecting the oral law demands *more* systematic legal reasoning, not literalism. Karaites (al-Qūmisī, Qirqisānī, the Tustarīs, the Jerusalem exegetes, al-Baṣīr) and Rabbanites are each shown as internally diverse.
- **Kabbalah (V23 correction):** three layers are kept apart: a pre-Kabbalistic text (*Sefer Yetzirah*), medieval Kabbalistic texts (Provence/Catalonia, the *Zohar*, Gikatilla) and later philosophical interpretations (Pico, Conway, modern readings of Ibn Gabirol). Following Weiss, there is no single Kabbalistic system, so every claim is limited to "the texts quoted here". A four-row table compares Maimonides with those texts on attributes, unity and multiplicity, creation, and knowledge and interpretation. The section notes that the opposition itself is being re-examined (Weiss citing Wolfson; Idel only via a review).
- **Spinoza:** the *TTP*'s rule that scripture is interpreted from scripture, against Maimonides. Only the break is treated; the metaphysics is left to EA2.
- **Mendelssohn, Buber, Rosenzweig, Levinas:** revealed law rather than doctrine; relation as revelation; philosophy beginning from death; the face as a demand that comes before choice. Arendt appears only as a contrast.

## 5. Critiques and responses

There are six topics:

1. Criticism of Maimonides' negative theology: Aquinas' objection in *ST* I q.13 and the "empty negation" worry.
2. Reason and revelation: Crescas against trust in Aristotle's physics.
3. Divine knowledge and human freedom: the three positions compared.
4. Spinoza's critique of religious authority and its reception.
5. Modern Jewish identity and secularisation: the controversy over Mendelssohn, and three answers.
6. Levinas: infinite responsibility to one other and justice in a community.

Each topic separates the historical criticism, or the documented discussion, from the chapter's "可能的回应" or "讨论".
