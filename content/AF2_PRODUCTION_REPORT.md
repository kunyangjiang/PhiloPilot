## 2. Important arguments

Diagne's defence of Senghor and what it leaves open; Césaire's "two ways to lose oneself"; Nyerere's recovery argument and its descriptive vs normative premises; Wiredu's two-part programme and his test "in one's own language"; Osha's and Bakare-Yusuf's point that a missing word proves less than it seems; Gyekye's selective inheritance and the question who selects; Mudimbe's "invention of Africa"; Fanon's refusal to treat precolonial society as a blueprint and the SEP criticisms of it; Appiah on African identity as historical; Oyěwùmí's language, history and norm claims separated, with what each can and cannot show.

## 7. Component

`ui/media/af2/DecolonizeCompare.tsx` (`decolonize-compare`), placed in 五 after the six-approach table and its following paragraph. Step 1: assign six moves (Senghor, Nyerere, Wiredu, Gyekye, Mudimbe, Fanon) to the six approaches. Step 2: for two cases (Wiredu on consensus democracy; Oyěwùmí on gender), classify each claim as linguistic, historical or normative evidence and read what it can and cannot show. Step 3: a view question. Justification: it practises the course's method directly — telling apart kinds of evidence for a decolonizing claim. Same accessibility pattern as the other components.
