## 2. Important arguments

1. **Critical theory (一).** Traditional vs critical theory; the two theses of *Dialectic of Enlightenment*; Adorno and Marcuse as two different directions; the standing question of the standard of critique.
2. **Structuralism (二).** Saussure's langue/parole, signifier/signified, arbitrariness, synchrony/diachrony, syntagmatic/associative relations, value and difference, each tied to a checked page of the 1971 Payot text; the movement beyond linguistics; the turn to post-structuralism.
3. **Foucault (三).** Archaeology and genealogy, disciplinary power, power/knowledge, and a table separating Foucault's claims from caricatures ("truth is only power"; the genetic fallacy). Objections on normativity and on historical evidence (the latter flagged as unverified).
4. **Derrida and Deleuze (四).** Presence and voice via Husserl; the two phases of deconstruction (reversal and reinscription); a worked example using Saussure's own French on writing (pp. 45, 51–52, 53, 165), read as the course's reconstruction from a common account; why deconstruction is not "anything goes". Deleuze's difference in itself is presented separately, with a comparison table and the instruction not to merge the two.
5. **Feminist theory (五).** Irigaray (sexual difference does not yet exist; mimicry), Kristeva (semiotic and symbolic), Butler (performativity, not voluntary performance; clarification from *Bodies That Matter* flagged as following the common account), Haraway (situated knowledges between the view from nowhere and relativism).
6. **Habermas (六).** Public sphere, communicative action and three validity claims, colonization of the lifeworld, his critique of Foucault and Derrida, discourse ethics as a standard of justification, and responses.

## 7. Component

**`writing-hierarchy`** (`src/ui/media/ea12/WritingHierarchy.tsx`), placed in 四 after the six-step deconstructive reading of Saussure on writing.

- **Why here.** "Deconstruction" is often taken as wordplay. Working with Saussure's own sentences shows that the reading depends on textual evidence: three passages set up the hierarchy of speech over writing, and one (p. 165) relies on writing to explain all signs.
- **What the reader does.** (1) Sorts four French passages, each shown with the chapter's translation, into "establishes the hierarchy" or "relies on writing"; (2) picks the conclusion of the deconstructive reading (reinscription, not reversal or refutation); (3) chooses between rival readings ("p. 165 is only an analogy", "the tension is real", "more text needed"), each with its strength and cost, no right answer.
- **Basis tags.** The closing claim is marked as the course's. The Limits panel states the transcription basis and that the page numbers were checked against the scan, that the reading is the course's reconstruction and not Derrida's words, and that Derrida's reading is contested (Daylight); text updated in §10.
- **Accessibility.** French marked `lang="fr"`; buttons with `aria-pressed`; verdicts in words; `aria-live` feedback; no animation. State saved under `philopilot.v2.ea12.media.writing-hierarchy`.
