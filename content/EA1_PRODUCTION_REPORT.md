## 2. Important arguments

- **Augustine:** emanation vs free creation *ex nihilo*. Evil as privation is reconstructed against Manichaean dualism. Time is distentio animi, starting from "if no one asks me, I know".
- **Eriugena (V23 correction):** "nature" covers what is and what is not; the fourfold division is set out in a table, and the fourth (neither creates nor is created) is God as end. God is "superessential", "nothing" by excellence rather than privation, and does not know *what* he is. Creation as theophany through the primordial causes, *ex nihilo* read as the divine nothing; the immanence–transcendence tension is named. A Plotinus–Augustine–Eriugena table, and his reception (the 1210/1225 condemnations, Eckhart, Cusanus) place him between late antiquity and scholasticism.
- **Boethius:** simple vs conditional necessity. God's knowledge is a "present seeing" from eternity, so the answer depends on God being outside time; this dependence is stated, and its limit is flagged.
- **Anselm:** an eight-step reductio. The criticisms are pinned to steps: the island attacks the form; Gaunilo attacks steps 2–3; Aquinas step 2; Kant step 6. The SEP reading of Anselm's reply is labelled as one interpretation.
- **Universals:** Abelard's contrary-properties argument; *status* as the common cause; Avicenna's "horseness is only horseness". Four positions are set out, each with its unpaid debt.
- **Aquinas:** only Ways 1–3 are cosmological (per SEP). They exclude simultaneous ("vertical") series, not temporal regress. "And this all call God" is read as cautious wording, with the attributes argued later. Aquinas rejects Anselm's argument.
- **Universities (V23 correction):** only how institutions shaped philosophical practice. The 1215 Courçon statute (no lectures on Aristotle's metaphysics and natural philosophy) and the 1255 statute (Aristotle's works as set texts, with fixed lecture times). Lectio produced commentaries, disputatio the *quaestio* form and the *Summa* article (described "依据通行的介绍"), the quodlibet, and Sentences commentaries; following Sweeney, "the institutionalisation of conflict" and the constraints of form; the 1277 condemnation following Thijssen. A practice→genre→effect table.
- **Scotus and Ockham:** univocity versus analogy; *haecceitas*. The razor's conditions are reason, experience and authority. Ockham's main ground against realism is incoherence.
- **Hildegard (V23 correction):** her own texts (the *Scivias* Protestificatio on how she sees, understands and was told to write; the *Liber divinorum operum* I.1 voice of the "fiery power" giving life to all things) are distinguished from the later *Vita* by Godfrey and Theoderic, which stresses that she had no human teaching. Following Rabassó: her contrast with "human science", the letter to Odo, the context of Gilbert of Poitiers and Bernard at Reims 1148, her absorption of Calcidius' *Timaeus*, the critical edition's parallels (Rabanus 25, the *Periphyseon* 15), and macrocosm and microcosm. Three things a reader can check are named.
- **Héloïse (V23 correction):** not as Abelard's biographical companion. Ep. II: guilt lies in intention, not effect; *meretrix*/*imperatrix*; fortune vs virtue. Ep. IV: outward chastity vs the mind; she took the veil at his command, not for God. Ep. VI: institutional rules vs moral responsibility, *indifferentia*, reconstructed in three steps. The *Problemata*. The authorship question is stated (Marenbon refuted sole Abelardian authorship; the anonymous love letters remain "hotly contested") and the Dronke/Mews/Marenbon/Doyle debate on the origin of intention ethics is presented without a verdict. A summary table compares her with Hildegard, and the women's exclusion from universities links back to §五.

## 5. Critiques and responses

There are five topics:

1. Criticisms of Anselm's argument: Gaunilo, Aquinas, Descartes, Kant and Russell's remark, with Anselm's reply.
2. Universals and nominalism: Abelard's objections, and a realist's possible reply.
3. Aquinas' proofs: why no infinite series, and why the first cause would be God.
4. Faith and reason: the text is not reduced to "reason subordinated to faith".
5. Scotus vs Ockham: two Franciscans on opposite sides over universals.

Historical criticism is kept apart from "可能的回应" or "讨论".
