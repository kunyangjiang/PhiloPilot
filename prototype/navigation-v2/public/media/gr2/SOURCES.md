### clarke-plato-euthyphro-1r.jpg

- **Object:** Plato, *Tetralogies 1–6* (24 dialogues, beginning with the
  *Euthyphro*), with marginal scholia: "the Clarke Plato". Bodleian Library,
  Oxford, **MS. E. D. Clarke 39**, fol. 1r: the opening of the *Euthyphro*
  (2a). The first line legible on the image reads τί νεώτερον, ὦ Σώκρατες,
  γέγονεν, ὅτι σὺ τὰς ἐν Λυκείῳ κατα[λιπὼν] …, matching Euthyphro 2a in the
  Perseus text (checked 2026-11-07).
- **Date and what it shows:** dated 895; Byzantine, Constantinople; scribe
  "Johannes calligraphus"; commissioned by Arethas of Patrae (later bishop of
  Caesarea), who added scholia. Made some twelve centuries after Plato's death.
  It is evidence of how the text was transmitted, not of Plato's own
  autograph. The caption says this.
- **Holder:** Bodleian Libraries, University of Oxford. Record (Digital
  Bodleian, read 2026-11-07):
  <https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/f57d074b-cff1-4172-8236-797c7b8f0403/>;
  its IIIF manifest gives shelfmark, date (895), place (Byzantine,
  Constantinople), scribe, the Arethas commission and the description "the
  oldest manuscript (discounting papyrus fragments) for about half the
  dialogues of Plato". The catalogue record it links
  (`medieval.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/catalog/manuscript_4004`) was behind a bot
  check and was not opened.
- **Why not the Bodleian's own photograph:** the manifest states that the
  library's photographs are licensed **CC BY-NC 4.0**. They were not used, to
  avoid adding non-commercial terms to the course (as for GR1).
- **Image actually used, and its rights:** the plate "1 r." from the
  photographic facsimile *Codices graeci et latini photographice depicti*,
  t. 3: *Plato. Codex Oxoniensis Clarkianus 39* (Lugduni Batavorum:
  A. W. Sijthoff, 1898; preface by T. W. Allen, per the Commons description
  of the same plate). Scan by Columbia University Libraries, Internet Archive
  item `ldpd_10972550_003` (item metadata read 2026-11-07: publisher
  Sijthoff, volume "3 - Plato. Codex oxoniensis Clarkianus 39"; no rights
  restriction recorded). **Public domain:** the manuscript is medieval; the
  facsimile photographs were published in 1898 (in the public domain in the
  United States as a pre-1931 publication; a faithful two-dimensional
  reproduction of a public-domain work, and in any case more than 125 years
  old). Wikimedia Commons carries the same plate as
  *File:Euthyphron beginning. Clarke Plato.jpg* under {{PD-Art}}; the course
  file was taken from the Columbia scan, not from Commons.
- **File:** downloaded 2026-11-07 with Product Owner approval from
  `https://archive.org/download/ldpd_10972550_003/page/n23_w1000.jpg`
  (the Internet Archive page-image service; the leaf was identified by its
  plate label "1 r." and by comparison with the Commons file). 1088 × 1200 px
  greyscale JPEG, 188,538 bytes (matching the Content-Length header). Not
  altered (no crop, resize or colour change; the white margin and plate label
  are part of the scan).
- **Used in:** `src/ui/media/gr2/ClarkePlato.tsx` (section 一), via
  `CLARKE_PLATO` in `src/ui/media/assets.ts`.

### plato-head-mc1377.jpg

- **Object:** marble portrait head of Plato, Roman, 1st century CE; fine-grained
  white marble; head 34 cm high; from the sacred area of Largo Argentina, Rome.
  **Musei Capitolini, Rome, inv. MC 1377** (shown at the Centrale
  Montemartini).
- **Date and what it shows:** made some four centuries after Plato's death
  (347 BCE). It is at most a Roman copy of an earlier Greek portrait type; it
  is not a lifetime portrait. The photographer's Commons description
  attributes the type to Silanion's portrait for the Academy (ca. 370 BCE);
  the course reports this attribution as such and has not checked it. The
  caption says this.
- **Holder and identification:** the Musei Capitolini / Centrale Montemartini
  website was searched on 2026-11-07 and **no object record for MC 1377 was
  found**. Identification, inventory number, date, material, size and
  provenance were checked instead against the object entry of the Museo
  Galileo exhibition *Archimede* (Rome, Musei Capitolini):
  <https://mostre.museogalileo.it/archimede/oggetto/RitrattoPlatone.html>
  ("Ritratto di Platone … I secolo d.C. … Roma, Musei Capitolini, inv. MC
  1377"). This is a museum's exhibition record, not the holder's own
  collection record (see Open questions).
- **Rights:** the sculpture is in the public domain. The **photograph** is by
  Marie-Lan Nguyen (2009), licensed **CC BY 2.5**
  (<https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.5/>), as stated on
  <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Plato_Silanion_Musei_Capitolini_MC1377.jpg>
  (Commons API `extmetadata`, read 2026-11-07: LicenseShortName "CC BY 2.5",
  Artist "Marie-Lan Nguyen", Credit "Own work"). Attribution is required and
  is given in the caption ("Photo © Marie-Lan Nguyen / Wikimedia Commons,
  CC BY 2.5", with a link to the licence and the file page). Commercial use
  is allowed; there is no share-alike or non-commercial condition.
- **File:** downloaded 2026-11-07 with Product Owner approval from
  `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/Plato_Silanion_Musei_Capitolini_MC1377.jpg/960px-Plato_Silanion_Musei_Capitolini_MC1377.jpg`
  (a Wikimedia-generated 960 px thumbnail of the 2400 × 3600 original).
  960 × 1440 px JPEG, 305,614 bytes (matching the Content-Length header).
  Not altered by the course (the thumbnail is Wikimedia's own resize). Under

## Open questions

- **MC 1377:** the holder's own collection record was not found. The
  Product Owner may want to accept the Museo Galileo exhibition record as
  sufficient, or ask for the Musei Capitolini record before release.
- **Silanion attribution:** reported from the photographer's description
  only. If the caption should say more about the portrait type, a specialist
  source is needed.
- The facsimile's publication year is given here as 1898 (from the Internet
  Archive item description "t. 3-4. Plato. Codex oxoniensis Clarkianus 39.
  1898-99"); the item's own date field says 1897, which is the series' first
  year.
